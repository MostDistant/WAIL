# Sender-relative rounds: the room is a NINJAM room, not a clock

Status: accepted (supersedes ADR-0003 decisions 2 and 4 and ADR-0006; withdraws PR #508's draft "room-authoritative tempo" ADR; implementation follows)

WAIL grew a tower defending one guarantee. ADR-0003 gave the relay an authoritative interval clock so every peer agrees on the round number by construction. Defending that agreement spawned, in order: the `RoomLabeler` and its off-by-one hazard class, the relay's label watchdog, ADR-0006's grid alignment (entry snap, gated slew, δ measurement, a 25 ms target), and a tempo-threshold stack (report bar, hold-down, settling gate — culminating in PR #508's enforcement proposal) protecting the clock from re-anchor churn, because every tempo change re-anchors the clock and every re-anchor can shift a peer's labels by a whole interval. Each layer was a rational defense of the layer below.

On 2026-08-01 a design review took the tower apart with measurements and source reading, and found the foundation unoccupied:

- **Phase never reaches the ear.** Capture stores audio as beats-from-interval-start on the sender's grid (`internal/interval` `FrameOffset`); playout releases it at the receiver's own boundary (`internal/playout`). Sender beat 3 lands on receiver beat 3 regardless of how far apart the two grids sit in absolute time. The alignment requirement for correctness is only that grids agree within **half an interval** — seconds — so the index rounding is unambiguous; the 25 ms pursuit served diagnostics and comfort, not the music.
- **NINJAM ships with zero cross-client sync and has worked for twenty years** (source read, `njclient.cpp`): the grid is the sound card's sample counter (`:794-825`), the only shared musical state is two integers applied at the next local boundary (`:1112`, `:724-731`, `:792-810`), and playback puts each streamed interval on the receiver's next boundary once ~8 KB has arrived (`:462`, `:3120-3127`), with freshest-wins deletion bounding backlog (`:1305`). Its delay is grid phase luck in (0, 1] intervals, wandering with network weather — the folkloric "fixed one interval" does not exist even in NINJAM.
- **Link's tempo rule survives the WAN; its clock sync does not.** Timeline arbitration is a priority comparison on a monotonic beat-origin counter with no timing assumption (`Sessions.hpp:224`); simulated over the relay at 100–300 ms RTT it converges in one RTT, duplicates and echoes are structurally inert, and near-simultaneous conflicting changes resolve without split-brain given an owner-id tie-break (`wail-app/tempo_sim_wan_test.go`). Link's own time transfer volleys pings under a 50 ms per-exchange deadline and gives up after five misses (`Measurement.hpp:116`) — at WAN latency it refuses outright, which is the hard number behind ADR-0006's rejection of tunneling.
- **The owner call**: WAIL jams are NINJAM-style layering. Nobody makes coordinated, this-round-exactly changes. The shared round number — the guarantee the entire tower defends — has no customer.

**Decision: shrink the cross-WAN musical contract to NINJAM's, plus a per-sender counter that never needs agreement.**

1. **Shared state is two values: the tempo number and the interval length** (BPI = launch quantization, ADR-0004). Nothing else crosses the WAN as a correctness requirement.
2. **Rounds are sender-relative.** Senders stamp their own local interval index on WAIF frames — the wire format is unchanged, the number's meaning changes, and the sender-side room-label rewrite (`RewriteWaifIntervalIndex`) retires. There is no room index.
3. **Playback adopts NINJAM's scheduler**, with two WAIL retentions. Each sender's round plays at the receiver's next boundary once enough has arrived; a fresher complete round supersedes a stale queued one (bounding staleness by skipping); seams are crossfaded, late tails live-append as today. Retention one: rounds advance **per sender, not per channel** — one musician's streams never split across rounds (NINJAM's per-channel queues allow that). Retention two: **the archive stays complete** — a skipped round goes to the recorder, never silently discarded. Delivery remains TCP and loss-free; "loss" exists only at the speakers, exactly as in NINJAM.
4. **Tempo changes travel as declarations arbitrated by Link's own rule**, run WAN-side over the relay (the dual-session model: WAIL is a peer of its LAN Link session and of a Link-like WAN session of WAILs). A declaration carries the tempo and a monotonic origin stamp; strictly-greater adopts, lowest owner id breaks the exact ties a relay window produces (`Sessions.hpp:163` at session merge is Link's own precedent). No report threshold, hold-down, or enforcement is load-bearing: with no clock to defend, a spurious tempo message costs a millisecond-scale boundary splice, not a room-wide label re-roll. Denoising of DAW-observed changes survives only as politeness (the windowed-mean detector). WAIL's UI tempo control becomes a first-class declaration path — today `ChangeBpm` sets the local session and never reaches the room at all, a defect this work found.
5. **Retired**: the relay room clock, `interval_anchor`, and label watchdog (`roomclock.go`, `interval_clock.go`, `labelwatch.go`); `RoomLabeler` and the post-snap label re-derivation; grid alignment as a correctness mechanism — entry snap, grid slew, and the δ thresholds (δ may survive as observability only; the audible join snap's job is gone, so it goes). The interval offset D as a user-facing constant (env var, Debug slider) retires with the pinned schedule: delay becomes adaptive, which is NINJAM's actual behavior. The local grid remains Link's — that was never ours.

## Considered options (rejected)

- **Keep the relay-authoritative round (status quo).** Its only remaining customer was coordinated same-round changes, which the owner ruled out as a use case. Everything it costs is enumerated above, and its hazard class (labels off by one, healed by watchdog, re-rolled by anchors) has consumed weeks of field debugging.
- **Room-authoritative tempo with enforcement** (PR #508's draft ADR). Killed twice over: measured, correcting an insistent tempo source loses to adopting it 47:1 (`tempo_sim_shapes_test.go`, shape 9 — the #424 two-enforcer lesson, now quantified); and with no clock to defend, the premise evaporates. Withdrawn.
- **Sticky per-sender pin** (loss-free playback, fixed staleness). Fully designed during this review: pin at first arrival with a margin floor, never re-pin while audio flows, re-pin in silence gaps. Workable — but it trades NINJAM's field-proven musical continuity (the layer never gaps; it slides a round and slides back) for rhythmic stability (stable rounds with silence holes), and layered jamming wants continuity. Kept here as the documented fallback if adaptive staleness proves annoying in the field.
- **Room tempo follows a divergent LAN's mean.** Spectacular for one wobbling LAN (measured 106 ms → 1.6 ms mean misalignment), catastrophic for three (127 re-anchors and 702 ms heard skew in ten minutes) — last-writer-wins on a value several peers disagree about. The dissolution of the room clock makes the question moot: with rounds sender-relative, a LAN's tempo wobble harms only its own splices.
- **Tunnel Link's sync across the WAN** — re-rejected with the number ADR-0006 lacked: a 50 ms deadline × 5 attempts means Link does not degrade at WAN latency, it never joins.

## Consequences

- **Deletions** (verified consumer sweep, 2026-08-01): `signaling-server/{roomclock,interval_clock,labelwatch}.go`; `internal/interval/labeler.go` + the labeler-hazard and anchorsim tests (they test the retired model); the steering surface of `internal/align`; the anchor/label plumbing through `session.go` and the audio engine (`SetRoomAnchor`, `AlignRoomLabel`, `RoomIndex`); `RewriteWaifIntervalIndex`. The relay keeps room config (tempo/BPI) as plain state for joiners — NINJAM's `ConfigChangeNotify`, not a clock.
- **The off-by-one class dissolves rather than gets fixed** — with no correct round, a one-off pin is indistinguishable from grid luck. This includes the parked tier2 systematic off-by-one. `tier2-e2e` placement assertions rework to: content beat-aligned on the receiver grid, delay within the adaptive window, archive complete.
- **Pillars amended.** Pillar 2 loses "fixed": the delay is bounded by the interval and adaptive, which is what NINJAM actually does; still intervalic, still never chasing sub-20 ms. Pillar 3 narrows: capture is completely *delivered and archived*; playback prefers freshness. Pillar 4 stands — the local Link session is authoritative for tempo — and its two ADR-0006 carve-outs retire.
- **A protocol addition**: the tempo declaration message (tempo, origin stamp, owner). `StateSnapshot` tempo adoption and its arbitration retire with it.
- **What survives from the 2026-08-01 measurement work**: both simulation harnesses (`tempo_sim*.go`) as regression cover and as the instrument for the constants this ADR leaves open; the windowed-mean detector as politeness; the episodeBase slew fix, which remains correct for the shipped system until the retirement lands.
- **Open small designs for implementation**: the prebuffer/readiness constant; crossfade length at round seams; a per-round silence marker so an idle sender's queue advances (NINJAM's zero-GUID mechanism, `njclient.cpp:1271-1283`); join-time seeding of tempo/BPI from relay room state; and the UI tempo control (required — it is the declaration path).
- **PR #508 is withdrawn.** Its two factual corrections to ADR-0006 (the slew-cap history; grid jumps firing entry mid-session) remain accurate for the shipped system and should land as plain amendments there.
